# 04 — Viewing Keys and Responsible Disclosure

## Spending keys versus viewing keys

A spending key authorises funds to move and must remain secret. A viewing key can grant inspection rights without granting spending authority. This enables selective disclosure for auditing, personal bookkeeping, donations, or demonstrating receipt of funds.

Capabilities vary. A key may reveal incoming notes, outgoing notes, or only a restricted scope. Read the wallet documentation before sharing one.

## A consent-first workflow

1. State why disclosure is needed.
2. Define the time period and transactions.
3. Choose the least powerful key or export supported by the wallet.
4. Confirm the recipient and secure channel.
5. Record when access should end and revoke or rotate where possible.

Never treat a viewing key as harmless public data. It can reveal sensitive financial history.

## Compliance is contextual

Businesses, schools, charities, and regulated entities may have record-keeping or reporting duties. Privacy technology does not remove those duties. A responsible design supports lawful disclosure without making everyone’s history public.

For this youth programme, facilitators should avoid collecting identity documents or financial records. Questions about South African regulation belong with qualified legal or compliance professionals.

## Design exercise

Design a donation dashboard that lets a treasurer verify incoming payments without seeing unrelated household spending. Identify the minimum disclosure, consent screen, retention period, and deletion process.
