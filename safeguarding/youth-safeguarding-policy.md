# ZEBL Cape Town Youth Safeguarding Policy

## Purpose and scope

This policy protects participants aged 16–22 during ZEBL Cape Town workshops, online spaces, travel associated with the programme, and project demonstrations. It applies to organisers, mentors, volunteers, speakers, and participants.

## Core commitments

- Treat every learner with dignity, without discrimination or harassment.
- Obtain parent/guardian consent for participants aged 16–17.
- Use two-adult visibility for supervision; avoid isolated one-to-one meetings with minors.
- Collect the minimum personal data needed for attendance and safety.
- Keep consent, emergency, and accessibility records confidential.
- Never request seed phrases, spending keys, passwords, identity documents, or real-money transfers.

## Code of conduct

Participants and adults must not threaten, bully, sexually harass, exploit, dox, or discriminate against another person. Do not share another learner’s photo, address, wallet address, code, or story without permission. Technical curiosity is welcome; attempting to access another person’s device, account, wallet, or endpoint is not.

## Online and device safety

- Use official software sources and verify testnet network labels.
- Keep devices locked and updated.
- Do not install unknown wallet APKs or browser extensions.
- Report suspicious links, impersonation, lost devices, or exposed secrets immediately.
- If a secret is exposed, stop using it, tell a facilitator privately, and create a fresh testnet wallet.

## On-site security

Have a sign-in/out process, visible staff identification, accessible venues, first-aid and emergency contacts, safe transport guidance, and a quiet space. Do not publish the venue’s detailed security layout. Obtain permission before photography and do not publish identifiable information about minors.

## Reporting and response

A participant can report a concern to any facilitator, the designated safeguarding lead, or the emergency contact listed at registration. Facilitators should listen calmly, avoid promising secrecy, record factual details, preserve relevant evidence, and escalate promptly to the safeguarding lead. Immediate danger should be referred to local emergency services. Retaliation against a reporter is prohibited.

Safeguarding reports are handled on a need-to-know basis, with appropriate referral to parents/guardians and relevant professional or statutory services. This policy does not replace South African child-protection law or professional advice.

## Review

The organiser reviews this policy before each cohort, after any incident, and at least annually. Facilitators receive a short briefing on boundaries, consent, digital safety, and incident reporting before working with participants.
